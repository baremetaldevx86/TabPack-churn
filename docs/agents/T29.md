# T29 — Scientific limitations and threats to validity

## Assignment

Enumerate the scientific limitations of the reduced Churn-only comparison
(ordinary MLP, homogeneous independent MLP ensemble, and reduced heterogeneous
packed TabPack-style ensemble) relative to the TabPack paper. Define the claims
that the experiment can and cannot support.

## Sources and evidence

- `swarm.md`, Decisions 6–8: the provisional reduction uses seeds `[0, 1, 2]`,
  four base members, at most four selected members, at most 100 epochs,
  patience 16, batch size 256, float32, fixed baseline MLP settings, a small
  heterogeneous search space, no periodic feature embeddings, no Muon, no base
  pruning, and no tuning. It is explicitly labelled an optimistic reduced
  evaluation rather than a paper-equivalent score.
- TabPack paper v1 §3.5–§3.7 and §4.2, <https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05380v1>:
  the method samples many base models, constructs an online validation-greedy
  ensemble, and distinguishes optimistic reruns from a conservative tune-once
  evaluation.
- Paper v1 Appendix C: the authors identify repeated validation use as a source
  of possible overfitting, greater GPU memory use, and the engineering cost of
  implementing packed variants of layers and optimizers.
- Paper v1 Appendix E Table 5: Churn is one dataset with 6,400 train, 1,600
  validation, and 2,000 test rows, 7 numeric, 3 binary, and 1 categorical
  feature, and a binary-classification target. The paper’s main comparison
  spans 17 datasets and reports ten random seeds in Appendix G (including the
  Churn row).

## Findings: limitations and threats to validity

### 1. External validity is intentionally narrow

One Churn dataset cannot establish that a result transfers to other tabular
domains, sample sizes, feature mixtures, class balances, missingness patterns,
or temporal/IID regimes. The paper’s evidence covers 17 datasets, including
classification and regression and substantially larger feature and row
counts. A Churn result supports only a dataset-specific observation about this
implementation and split. It cannot support claims such as “TabPack is
competitive on tabular data” or a paper-wide rank.

### 2. The reduced capacity and candidate pool are not the paper method’s scale

Four candidates and a maximum four-member ensemble are much smaller than the
paper’s default pool of 64 and maximum ensemble size of 32. The reduction
changes the probability of sampling a useful configuration, the diversity of
the candidate pool, the number of validation comparisons, and the value of
greedy selection. It also prevents conclusions about the paper’s scalability,
large-pool selection behavior, or claimed packed throughput. The 100-epoch and
patience budget is another potentially material difference if convergence
depends on the larger pool or feature representation.

### 3. The model and optimizer recipe omits important paper components

The reduced run intentionally excludes periodic feature embeddings and Muon,
uses AdamW, and does not perform base pruning. The paper reports that feature
embeddings can materially affect performance and that Muon outperformed AdamW
in its experiments. Consequently, any observed gap may measure the chosen
small MLP/AdamW recipe rather than packed ensembling, and any failure to match a
paper number is not evidence against TabPack. Conversely, a gain in the
reduced comparison cannot be attributed to the full paper configuration.

### 4. The sampled hyperparameter distribution is a new experimental factor

The reduced depths, widths, dropout values, learning-rate range, and weight-
decay range are not the paper’s full search configuration. Fixed width/depth
and omitted embedding hyperparameters further alter the candidate population.
The result is conditional on these ranges and their random draws. A different
range, distribution, or sample seed could change both individual models and
which snapshots greedy selection chooses. The experiment should therefore
report the resolved per-member configurations, rather than describing the
outcome as tuning-free evidence for the paper’s default search space.

### 5. The optimistic protocol is not the paper’s conservative comparison

The provisional protocol reruns the complete four-member pack for each of three
seeds and evaluates the resulting selected ensemble. This is the optimistic
evaluation named in paper §4.2. The paper’s conservative protocol first makes
a main selection run, then evaluates only the selected base models across
secondary seeds, while charging the main-run runtime. Thus the reduced result
has a different selection/evaluation relationship and may benefit from fresh
candidate sampling in every seed. It must not be compared numerically to the
paper’s conservative values or described as reproducing its tune-once routine.

### 6. Three seeds provide descriptive, not strong inferential, evidence

Three paired seeds are useful for a smoke-scale estimate but are insufficient
for stable uncertainty claims about a small AUC difference. Seed variation can
be substantial for initialization, member sampling, batch order, dropout, and
checkpoint selection. Report every prespecified seed, mean and standard
deviation (and preferably paired per-seed differences); do not report the best
seed or imply statistical significance. The paper’s Churn table uses ten
seeds, so its spread is not directly comparable to a three-seed spread.

### 7. Validation is searched adaptively and can be overfit

The same validation split drives checkpointing, early stopping, and greedy
member selection. Online candidates also include multiple epochs/snapshots of
the same member. The selected validation AUC is therefore an adaptive maximum
over correlated trials, not an unbiased generalization estimate. Keep the test
split untouched until the ensemble, checkpoint, stopping epoch, and seed list
are frozen; evaluate test exactly once per completed seed. Record candidate
counts, selection traces, and stopping epochs so the validation search is
auditable. If development has already been guided by test results, no existing
test number should be called confirmatory without a newly locked holdout.

### 8. The single split limits split-level generalization

Even if the canonical paper split is preserved, one train/validation/test split
does not quantify split uncertainty or robustness to temporal drift, prevalence
shift, or alternate sampling. Churn is especially susceptible to operational
and cohort shifts. A fixed split is appropriate for a controlled reproduction,
but the scope should say “on the canonical Churn split,” not “for Churn in
general.” Any deviation in source file, row order, target coding, or split
identity is a further provenance threat and must fail validation rather than be
silently substituted.

### 9. Accuracy gains are confounded by capacity and compute accounting

An ordinary MLP has one member, while both ensemble comparisons can use four
members and heterogeneous packed tensors are padded to maximum width/depth.
Improvements may therefore reflect more trainable parameters, more prediction
averaging, or a larger effective compute budget rather than packing itself.
Report active parameter counts, padded capacity, member count, training steps,
and an explicitly stated wall-clock/FLOP convention for all three families.
Equal epoch and batch settings do not imply equal optimization work. A packed
speedup also cannot be inferred from accuracy results alone.

### 10. Baseline tuning and feature parity differ from the paper

The reduced baseline is fixed by the provisional configuration and has no
Optuna-style tuning, while the paper’s conventional baselines generally use
tuning and multi-seed evaluation. The reduced comparison also omits the
paper’s broader baselines (for example XGBoost, TabM, foundation models, and
embedding variants). Therefore it is a controlled comparison among three
locally implemented families, not a fair re-ranking of all paper methods.
If a baseline receives a different preprocessing, checkpoint, selection, or
compute budget, the resulting gap is a protocol confound rather than a method
effect; the same split, train-only preprocessing, seed mapping, stopping rule,
and test-after-freeze rule should be applied to all three families.

### 11. Preprocessing and target provenance can change the comparison

The reduced contract uses train-only numeric median imputation and quantile
normalization plus train-only categorical mode imputation and one-hot encoding
with unknown categories ignored. The paper describes a modified quantile
normalization with dataset-specific exceptions and treats binary features as
categorical. Small differences in imputation, quantile implementation, binary
handling, category vocabulary, target encoding, or split filtering can change
the AUC. The run manifest must identify the exact dataset hash, split counts,
target mapping, preprocessing fit scope, and transformed dimensions. IBM Telco,
synthetic, or otherwise convenient Churn data cannot stand in for the paper’s
Churn dataset.

### 12. Local runtime and memory do not establish paper efficiency

The paper’s main experiments use an NVIDIA A100 80GB, whereas this project is
being developed on a Windows laptop with optional CUDA and CPU fallback. GPU
kernel fusion, memory bandwidth, driver/PyTorch versions, and CPU/GPU launch
overhead can reverse relative timings, especially for only four members.
Report local wall time and peak memory as environment-specific measurements;
do not generalize them to A100, MacBook, or paper-wide efficiency claims.

### 13. Independent reimplementation adds construct validity risk

The project is independently authored and must not execute or import the
official implementation. Even with packed-forward and optimizer equivalence
tests, differences in initialization, dropout masks, optimizer state, loss
reduction, batch permutations, masking, checkpoint cloning, or AUC tie handling
can produce behavior unlike the paper. Passing local tests establishes the
declared reduced contract, not equivalence to the full pinned code. The report
should call this a “TabPack-style” packed ensemble and list implementation
deviations explicitly.

### 14. AUC alone does not establish operational utility

ROC-AUC is threshold-independent and can hide calibration, precision/recall at
an operating threshold, subgroup disparity, and the business cost of false
retention or false churn alerts. The reduced scope may report additional binary
metrics, but no operational claim should be made without predeclared metrics,
class-prevalence context, and threshold/calibration analysis. This is a
scientific scope limitation, not a reason to tune on the test set.

## Claim boundary for the final report

Safe wording is:

> “On the canonical Churn split, under the declared three-seed, four-member
> reduced protocol, the independently implemented heterogeneous packed MLP
> produced [observed result] relative to the ordinary and homogeneous MLP
> baselines. Checkpointing, stopping, and greedy selection used validation AUC;
> the test split was evaluated only after selection was frozen.”

The result is exploratory and dataset/protocol-specific. It may demonstrate
that the local packed implementation executes and provide a controlled local
comparison, but it does not reproduce the paper’s reported scores, validate
the paper’s multi-dataset ranking, establish superiority over tuned baselines,
or establish cross-hardware efficiency.

## Integration advice

- Keep the reduced/non-paper-equivalent label in the CLI report, README, and
  result artifacts.
- Require the manifest to record dataset/split identity, preprocessing scope,
  resolved member hyperparameters, per-seed outcomes, candidate-selection
  trace, active/padded capacity, device, and timing convention.
- Enforce the T47 rule: validation may select, but test is read only after the
  selection is frozen; report all three seeds rather than the winner.
- Treat comparisons to paper numbers as contextual reference only. A future
  paper-aligned study would need the paper’s dataset suite, feature embeddings,
  optimizer recipe, candidate counts, conservative evaluation, and seed budget.
